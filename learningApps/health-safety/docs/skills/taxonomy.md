# Health and Safety — Skills Taxonomy

Date: 2026-10-29

An audience-independent catalog of independently assessable competencies for maintaining health, preventing injury and disease, recognizing danger, obtaining appropriate help, and protecting oneself and others. Domain → Group → Skill denotes classification, not a grade, course sequence, assessment rubric, medical protocol, or mastery record. Curricula determine age-appropriate selection and depth; local services and procedures are contextualized in lessons.

**Boundaries:** This subject addresses practical prevention, safety decisions, self-care, and first response. Biology owns physiological and microbiological explanations; Psychology owns psychological mechanisms; Informatics owns technical cybersecurity; Economics owns market mechanisms; Engineering owns device design. Overlapping real-life situations are allowed where the health-and-safety competency differs. The “What to do if…?” format is a type of lesson or practice scenario, not a Skill. This taxonomy is educational, not individualized medical advice or a substitute for supervised practical first-aid training.

**Breadth references:** [WHO — Health-promoting schools](https://www.who.int/health-topics/health-promoting-schools), [WHO — Health literacy](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/health-literacy), [WHO — Self-care for health and well-being](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/self-care-health-interventions), [IFRC — International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025](https://www.ifrc.org/document/ifrc-international-first-aid-resuscitation-and-education-guidelines-2025), [UNESCO — International technical guidance on sexuality education](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000260770), and [UNICEF Ukraine — Mine safety for educators](https://www.unicef.org/ukraine/documents/teachers-to-pupils-about-mine-safety). These inform subject breadth, not a mandated national curriculum. Draft for editorial approval.

## DOMAINS

### Health Literacy and Care

Groups: 3 · Skills: 12

[Not implemented](#)

Personal health decisions rely on interpreting bodily changes, credible evidence, and the division of responsibility between self-care and professional services. This domain concerns informed choices and access to care rather than the biological mechanisms of disease.

#### Health and Risk

Skills: 5

[Not implemented](#)

Health depends on interacting physical, psychological, social, and environmental conditions. Risk factors affect probability rather than determining an individual's diagnosis.

##### Recognize changes in health status

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish a usual bodily variation from a new or worsening change that deserves monitoring or advice, without treating a single symptom as a definitive diagnosis.

##### Interpret basic health measurements

[Not implemented](#)

Relate observations such as temperature, pulse, or blood pressure to measurement conditions and reference context without making a diagnosis from one reading.

##### Identify factors affecting health

[Not implemented](#)

Connect habits, exposures, living conditions, heredity, and access to services with health outcomes while avoiding simplistic one-cause explanations.

##### Assess health-related risk

[Not implemented](#)

Evaluate the likelihood and potential consequences of longer-term health outcomes arising from exposures, behaviours or protective measures, accounting for uncertainty without converting population-level estimates into diagnoses.

##### Choose preventive health measures

[Not implemented](#)

Match health risks to evidence-based prevention such as vaccination, screening advice or protective daily habits, distinguishing the prevention of illness from first aid, clinical treatment and general accident avoidance.

#### Using Healthcare

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Seeking care effectively includes choosing an appropriate service, describing a concern, understanding an agreed plan, and knowing when assistance is urgent.

##### Choose an appropriate level of healthcare

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish routine consultation, timely professional assessment, and urgent emergency response by the nature and progression of symptoms.

##### Communicate symptoms to a clinician

[Not implemented](#)

Convey onset, duration, relevant changes, treatments already taken, and questions without substituting an assumed diagnosis for observations.

##### Understand a care plan

[Not implemented](#)

Interpret the purpose, timing, follow-up, and precautions of a clinician's instructions and clarify uncertainty before acting.

##### Participate in informed healthcare decisions

[Not implemented](#)

Recognize the roles of understandable information, consent, alternatives, privacy, and the right to ask questions in clinical encounters.

#### Health Information and Medicines

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

Claims about prevention and treatment should be evaluated against trustworthy evidence, and medicines require label-aware, safe handling; clinical observations and basic measurements are classified in Health and Risk.

##### Evaluate health information sources

[Not implemented](#)

Assess provenance, professional consensus, supporting evidence, conflicts of interest, and publication context rather than relying on popularity or testimonials.

##### Recognize misleading health claims

[Not implemented](#)

Identify miracle-cure promises, unsupported causal claims, and advertising disguised as medical advice, including claims about supplements.

##### Use medicines safely

[Not implemented](#)

Respect intended use, dosing instructions, contraindications, storage, expiry, and professional guidance; avoid sharing prescription medicine or guessing a dose.

### Hygiene and Infection Prevention

Groups: 3 · Skills: 12

[Not implemented](#)

Disease prevention includes reducing contact with infectious agents and maintaining sanitary conditions. This domain concerns personal and environmental prevention, not microbiological taxonomy or clinical infection treatment.

#### Personal Hygiene

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Effective routines reflect how contamination spreads between hands, body surfaces, shared objects, and everyday environments.

##### Hand hygiene

[Not implemented](#)

Use washing or an appropriate hand sanitizer at meaningful moments, paying attention to coverage and the circumstances in which soap and water are needed.

##### Oral hygiene

[Not implemented](#)

Maintain teeth and gums through suitable brushing, interdental care, and preventive dental visits while recognizing that pain or bleeding may require assessment.

##### Body hygiene

[Not implemented](#)

Select suitable cleaning and personal-care routines for skin, hair, and intimate areas without assuming excessive washing is beneficial.

##### Hygiene of personal items

[Not implemented](#)

Reduce contamination from shared towels, grooming tools, and reusable items through appropriate individual use and cleaning.

#### Transmission and Protection

Skills: 5

[Not implemented](#)

Infectious hazards can spread through different routes and require measures suited to the agent and environment, not one universal precaution.

##### Recognize infection transmission routes

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish respiratory, contact, foodborne, bloodborne, and vector-related exposure when considering prevention.

##### Respiratory hygiene

[Not implemented](#)

Reduce exposure when coughing, sneezing, or experiencing respiratory symptoms using appropriate etiquette, ventilation, and context-specific precautions.

##### Safe contact with blood and body fluids

[Not implemented](#)

Avoid direct unprotected exposure and understand when barriers, handwashing, and professional assistance are necessary.

##### Vaccination literacy

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish how immunization reduces specified disease risks from claims of absolute protection and recognize the role of recommended schedules.

##### Respond to signs of infectious illness

[Not implemented](#)

Decide when to rest, reduce exposure to others, monitor worsening symptoms, and seek professional advice rather than relying solely on home care.

#### Sanitary Environments

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

Water, indoor air, surfaces, and sanitation infrastructure influence exposure and should be managed according to actual contamination pathways.

##### Safe drinking water

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish potable from potentially contaminated supplies and recognize when verified treatment or an alternative source is required.

##### Indoor air and ventilation

[Not implemented](#)

Identify circumstances in which fresh-air exchange and source control reduce airborne exposure, while accounting for outdoor hazards.

##### Cleaning and disinfection

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish routine removal of dirt from targeted disinfection and avoid unsafe mixing or misuse of cleaning chemicals.

### Nutrition and Food Safety

Groups: 3 · Skills: 13

[Not implemented](#)

Eating and drinking support health through nutrients, adequate intake, and safe preparation. Nutritional choices and microbial or chemical food hazards are distinct competencies, even though the same meal can involve both.

#### Nutritional Foundations

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Food supplies energy and essential nutrients; dietary adequacy depends on patterns over time rather than judging a single meal as healthy or unhealthy.

##### Balanced dietary patterns

[Not implemented](#)

Consider variety, food groups, nutrient adequacy, and individual needs without assuming one fixed menu fits everyone.

##### Understand the roles of nutrients

[Not implemented](#)

Relate macronutrients, vitamins, minerals and fibre to their major physiological roles and distinguish nutritional adequacy from the amount of food alone, without reproducing biochemical mechanisms.

##### Hydration

[Not implemented](#)

Recognize fluid needs and common signs of insufficient intake while accounting for activity, climate, and health circumstances.

##### Interpret dietary energy balance

[Not implemented](#)

Relate energy intake to expenditure and changing individual needs while distinguishing energy balance from dietary quality and avoiding unsupported conclusions about a person's health from body size.

#### Food Choices and Information

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Marketing, accessibility, food labels, allergies, and personal circumstances shape real-life nutritional decisions.

##### Interpret food labels

[Not implemented](#)

Use ingredient lists, nutritional information, serving quantities, and date markings without treating a marketing slogan as evidence.

##### Recognize dietary misinformation

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish evidence-based nutritional advice from detox claims, extreme restrictions, and unsupported promises of rapid results.

##### Manage food allergy risks

[Not implemented](#)

Identify allergens and cross-contact warnings, verify ingredients, take appropriate avoidance precautions and understand when an allergic reaction requires urgent care.

##### Adapt food choices to dietary needs

[Not implemented](#)

Select suitable alternatives for medical, nutritional, ethical or cultural dietary requirements, distinguishing individual preference from a potentially dangerous allergen exposure.

#### Foodborne Hazards

Skills: 5

[Not implemented](#)

Food safety depends on separation, temperature, handling, and storage; appearance or smell alone cannot always establish whether food is safe.

##### Maintain hygiene during food preparation

[Not implemented](#)

Maintain clean hands, utensils and work surfaces while preparing meals, limiting microbial contamination without subsuming the distinct task of keeping raw foods separate from ready-to-eat foods.

##### Prevent raw-to-ready food cross-contamination

[Not implemented](#)

Keep raw animal products and their juices from contaminating ready-to-eat food through separate equipment, storage and handling; this concerns foodborne pathogens rather than allergen cross-contact.

##### Safe cooking and reheating

[Not implemented](#)

Relate sufficient heating to reducing specified foodborne hazards rather than relying only on colour or texture.

##### Safe food storage

[Not implemented](#)

Select appropriate storage conditions and time limits, distinguishing shelf-stable from perishable foods.

##### Recognize unsafe food situations

[Not implemented](#)

Identify spoilage, damaged packaging, unsafe water, and uncertain storage histories while understanding that some hazards are invisible.

### Movement, Sleep and Everyday Routines

[Not implemented](#)

Physical functioning benefits from feasible movement, adequate recovery, and adjustments to daily environments. This domain concerns practical health habits, not the physiology of exercise as a separate science.

#### Physical Activity

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Activity can vary by intensity, form, and personal ability; protective choices include gradual progression and responding to warning signals.

##### Plan regular physical activity

[Not implemented](#)

Combine suitable types and amounts of movement with individual capacity and opportunities, rather than relying on one sport or a universal target.

##### Prepare for physical exertion

[Not implemented](#)

Use context-appropriate warm-up, equipment, environment checks, and gradual increases in demand.

##### Recognize warning signs during activity

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish ordinary exertion from concerning symptoms or conditions requiring stopping, assistance, or medical assessment.

##### Prevent exercise-related injury

[Not implemented](#)

Adjust technique, load, recovery, and protective measures to the activity without assuming injury is unavoidable.

#### Sleep and Recovery

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

Sleep quality is affected by timing, environment, individual needs, and habits; persistent difficulty may warrant professional attention.

##### Understand sleep needs

[Not implemented](#)

Relate sufficient sleep and regular timing to functioning while accounting for differences across life stages and circumstances.

##### Support healthy sleep routines

[Not implemented](#)

Identify modifiable factors such as light, noise, stimulants, and schedule regularity that can affect sleep.

##### Recognize problematic sleep patterns

[Not implemented](#)

Notice persistent disruption or dangerous sleepiness and distinguish a need for evaluation from an occasional poor night.

#### Ergonomics and Sensory Care

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

Workspaces and devices can be adapted to reduce strain, fatigue, and exposure rather than enforcing a single rigid body position.

##### Adapt an ergonomic workspace

[Not implemented](#)

Adjust screen, seat, reach, lighting, and variation of posture to a person's task and physical needs.

##### Manage visual strain

[Not implemented](#)

Use suitable lighting, viewing distance, breaks, and symptom awareness during sustained near or screen work.

##### Protect hearing from noise

[Not implemented](#)

Relate loudness and exposure time to hearing risk, including personal listening devices and noisy environments.

### Mental Well-being and Support

Groups: 3 · Skills: 10

[Not implemented](#)

Health protection includes noticing psychological strain, choosing supportive coping strategies, and connecting people with qualified help. The psychological mechanisms of emotion and cognition belong principally to Psychology.

#### Recognizing Strain

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

Distress varies in intensity and duration; a sign can indicate a need for support without establishing a psychiatric diagnosis.

##### Recognize signs of stress

[Not implemented](#)

Connect changes in sleep, concentration, mood, behaviour, or bodily tension with potential overload while considering other causes.

##### Recognize warning signs of worsening mental health

[Not implemented](#)

Identify persistent impairment, escalating distress, or loss of usual functioning as reasons to seek suitable support.

##### Recognize harmful coping patterns

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish restorative actions from strategies that may intensify distress or introduce additional risks.

#### Everyday Coping and Assistance

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Helpful responses can involve self-care, support networks, appropriate boundaries, and referral rather than demanding that a person solve a crisis alone.

##### Use safe coping strategies

[Not implemented](#)

Select proportionate methods such as grounding, rest, movement, and social connection without presenting them as substitutes for necessary care.

##### Seek mental health support

[Not implemented](#)

Identify trusted people, professional services, and ways of communicating a concern, including when ordinary support is insufficient.

##### Support someone experiencing distress

[Not implemented](#)

Listen without judgment, respect privacy, avoid diagnosing, and help the person reach appropriate assistance.

##### Prevent chronic overload

[Not implemented](#)

Notice sustained imbalance between demands and recovery and identify feasible changes or sources of support.

#### Mental Health Crises

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

Immediate safety and connection to competent support take priority over attempting therapy or managing a potentially dangerous situation alone.

##### Recognize an acute psychological crisis

[Not implemented](#)

Identify immediate danger, severe disorientation, or expressed intent to self-harm as circumstances requiring prompt adult or professional help.

##### Respond to self-harm or suicide risk

[Not implemented](#)

Prioritize safety, timely emergency or crisis-service involvement, and staying within one's competence without secrecy promises.

##### Provide psychological first aid

[Not implemented](#)

Offer calm, practical, non-intrusive support, immediate needs assessment, and connection with services without forcing disclosure of traumatic experiences.

### Personal Boundaries and Violence Prevention

Groups: 3 · Skills: 13

[Not implemented](#)

Safety in relationships requires bodily autonomy, recognition of coercion, and effective routes to protection. Mechanisms of social influence are studied in Psychology; here the emphasis is on avoiding harm and obtaining help.

#### Bodily Autonomy and Consent

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

Personal space, privacy, and permission govern respectful contact; consent must not be inferred from silence, fear, or power imbalance.

##### Recognize personal boundaries

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish acceptable from unwanted contact and intrusion into personal space, possessions, or sensitive information.

##### Communicate refusal and limits

[Not implemented](#)

State a boundary or leave an unsafe interaction in a way that prioritizes personal safety over politeness.

##### Recognize valid consent

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish voluntary, informed, specific, and reversible permission from pressure or absence of resistance.

#### Pressure and Manipulation

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Coercion can use threats, secrecy, guilt, status, dependency, or urgency; a protective response need not persuade the aggressor.

##### Recognize psychological pressure

[Not implemented](#)

Identify demands reinforced by guilt, intimidation, isolation, or threatened consequences rather than genuine choice.

##### Resist unsafe peer pressure

[Not implemented](#)

Use refusal, disengagement, and supportive allies when a group promotes hazardous or humiliating behaviour.

##### Recognize grooming and exploitation

[Not implemented](#)

Identify boundary testing, forced secrecy, rewards, and escalating requests as possible patterns of exploitation without blaming the targeted person.

##### Set boundaries in unequal relationships

[Not implemented](#)

Account for authority, dependency, age, or financial power when judging whether a demand or apparent agreement is free.

#### Violence and Protection

Skills: 6

[Not implemented](#)

Violence can be physical, psychological, sexual, economic, or digital; protection and reporting should avoid exposing affected people to retaliation.

##### Recognize bullying

[Not implemented](#)

Identify a pattern of harmful repeated conduct involving a power imbalance, distinguishing bullying from a mutual disagreement or an isolated conflict.

##### Recognize harassment

[Not implemented](#)

Identify intimidating, degrading, discriminatory or otherwise unwanted targeted conduct, which may be serious even without a repeated bullying pattern.

##### Respond safely to bullying

[Not implemented](#)

Prioritize disengagement, trusted assistance, and reporting channels over retaliation or direct confrontation with aggressors.

##### Recognize domestic and sexual violence

[Not implemented](#)

Identify controlling, threatening, non-consensual, or abusive behaviour, including harm by familiar or trusted people.

##### Seek protection after violence

[Not implemented](#)

Identify safe contacts, support organizations, emergency services, and appropriate documentation without requiring disclosure to an unsafe person.

##### Support a person affected by violence

[Not implemented](#)

Respond without blame, respect the person's choices and immediate safety, and facilitate qualified support rather than investigating the incident.

### Substance Use and Dependence Prevention

[Not implemented](#)

Tobacco, alcohol, drugs, and other dependence-forming patterns can affect health and safety. The focus is recognizing hazards, resisting coercion, and seeking care rather than pharmacological synthesis or moral judgment.

#### Substance-Related Risks

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Effects depend on the substance, amount, context, interactions, and vulnerability; legal availability does not imply harmlessness.

##### Recognize tobacco and nicotine risks

[Not implemented](#)

Connect smoking, vaping, and other nicotine exposure with dependence and health harm, including second-hand exposure.

##### Recognize alcohol-related risks

[Not implemented](#)

Identify impaired judgment, coordination, poisoning risk, and longer-term health consequences associated with alcohol.

##### Recognize drug-related risks

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish unpredictable potency, impairment, overdose, and interactions without treating all substances as having identical effects.

##### Recognize medicine misuse

[Not implemented](#)

Identify hazardous use outside prescribed or labelled purposes, including sharing medication and mixing substances.

#### Prevention and Access to Help

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Dependence involves more than a single behaviour; early support and non-stigmatizing treatment pathways matter.

##### Recognize signs of problematic use

[Not implemented](#)

Notice loss of control, increasing harms, and neglected obligations as reasons for further assessment rather than a basis for labeling someone.

##### Respond to offers of harmful substances

[Not implemented](#)

Use refusal and exit strategies appropriate to pressure, power dynamics, and immediate safety.

##### Recognize behavioural dependence risks

[Not implemented](#)

Identify patterns of gambling or digital engagement that cause persistent harm without assuming ordinary enjoyment is addiction.

##### Seek help for substance-related problems

[Not implemented](#)

Identify trusted people and health or addiction services, including urgent assistance for suspected poisoning or overdose.

### Everyday and Occupational Safety

Groups: 5 · Skills: 19

[Not implemented](#)

Injuries often arise from foreseeable hazards in homes, workplaces, educational spaces, and public facilities. Preventive controls and safe equipment use take priority over reacting after an incident.

#### Hazard Recognition and Prevention

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Risk management involves identifying possible harm, checking exposure, and favouring elimination or protective controls over mere caution.

##### Identify hazards in an environment

[Not implemented](#)

Notice physical, chemical, electrical, and situational dangers without assuming a familiar place is automatically safe.

##### Evaluate injury risk

[Not implemented](#)

Evaluate how an identifiable hazard could cause immediate accidental injury, who may be exposed and how reliably existing controls reduce that danger; chronic disease-risk estimation belongs to Health and Risk.

##### Choose protective measures

[Not implemented](#)

Prefer avoiding or removing a hazard, safe design and procedure, and suitable protection rather than relying solely on warnings.

##### Respond to unsafe conditions

[Not implemented](#)

Pause hazardous activity, move to safety, notify a responsible person, and avoid attempting work beyond one's competence.

#### Household Hazards

Skills: 5

[Not implemented](#)

Ordinary domestic objects and spaces can cause falls, burns, poisoning, cuts, and injury when access or use is unsafe.

##### Prevent falls in living spaces

[Not implemented](#)

Identify slipping, tripping, unstable climbing, and unguarded height hazards and match them to safer arrangements.

##### Safe use of sharp objects

[Not implemented](#)

Apply appropriate handling, storage, and supervision to cutting or piercing tools while avoiding unnecessary exposure.

##### Prevent household poisoning

[Not implemented](#)

Reduce access to hazardous chemicals, use original labelled containers, and avoid unsafe storage near food or medicine.

##### Safe handling of hot surfaces and liquids

[Not implemented](#)

Recognize scalding and contact-burn hazards in kitchens, bathrooms, and heating areas.

##### Prevent entrapment and suffocation hazards

[Not implemented](#)

Identify risks from confined spaces, cords, bags, and unsafe storage for vulnerable people.

#### Personal Safety in Public Places

Skills: 2

[Not implemented](#)

Being approached by unknown people or separated from familiar companions calls for independent judgement about safe distance, exit options and trustworthy assistance, without assuming every unfamiliar person is dangerous.

##### Respond to unsafe approaches in public

[Not implemented](#)

Recognize unwanted invitations, requests for secrecy, pressure to leave a safe location or other suspicious approaches; maintain distance, decline unsafe requests and seek support from an appropriate trusted person or service.

##### Seek help when lost or separated

[Not implemented](#)

Use an appropriate safe location, identifiable helpers and reliable contact channels when separated from companions; avoid sharing unnecessary personal information or following an unverified stranger.

#### Electricity, Fire and Gas

Skills: 5

[Not implemented](#)

Electrical hazards, ignition and fire, fuel-gas leaks, carbon monoxide exposure and heating equipment require different prevention and emergency responses; intervening in faulty systems can endanger bystanders.

##### Electrical safety

[Not implemented](#)

Recognize damaged wiring, water-related hazards, overloaded circuits, and the limits of safe user action.

##### Fire prevention

[Not implemented](#)

Identify ignition sources, flammable materials, and preventive practices appropriate to the setting.

##### Gas leak safety

[Not implemented](#)

Recognize suspected leakage of combustible fuel gas and the risk of fire or explosion; withdraw from danger, seek appropriate assistance and avoid unsafe attempts to inspect or repair the system.

##### Safe use of heating equipment

[Not implemented](#)

Account for ventilation, clearance, stable installation, and manufacturer instructions rather than improvised operation.

##### Carbon monoxide safety

[Not implemented](#)

Understand that carbon monoxide from combustion may be invisible and odorless, recognize alarm or exposure concerns, and prioritize getting to safety and obtaining emergency evaluation rather than relying on smell.

#### Tools and Workplaces

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

Protective procedures depend on the activity, equipment, and conditions; professional work requires role-appropriate training.

##### Read safety signs and instructions

[Not implemented](#)

Interpret warning symbols, restricted areas, and equipment guidance in context instead of treating a label as complete protection.

##### Use personal protective equipment

[Not implemented](#)

Match protection to the hazard and fit, recognizing that protective equipment does not eliminate the hazard.

##### Follow safe equipment procedures

[Not implemented](#)

Respect guards, operating limits, maintenance restrictions, and stop conditions without attempting unauthorized repair.

### Transport, Water and Outdoor Safety

Groups: 3 · Skills: 13

[Not implemented](#)

Movement outside controlled environments combines traffic rules, environmental conditions, and limited opportunities for immediate rescue. This domain addresses safe decisions rather than vehicle engineering.

#### Road and Transport Safety

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Protection differs for pedestrians, riders, passengers, and drivers; visible signals and protective equipment do not remove every traffic risk.

##### Pedestrian road safety

[Not implemented](#)

Assess crossings, visibility, traffic movement, signals, and distractions before entering a roadway.

##### Cycling and micromobility safety

[Not implemented](#)

Use protective equipment, safe positioning, visibility, and context-appropriate road rules for bicycles and similar devices.

##### Passenger safety

[Not implemented](#)

Recognize the role of seat belts, appropriate child restraints, safe boarding, and behavior in moving vehicles.

##### Safe driving decisions

[Not implemented](#)

Recognize how fatigue, impairment, speed, distraction, and environmental conditions affect driving risk and the duty not to drive unsafely.

#### Water Safety

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

##### Assess swimming and bathing hazards

[Not implemented](#)

Assess water-specific conditions such as depth, currents, temperature, supervision, safe access and local warnings before entering; this applies general hazard judgment to an environment with distinct drowning risks.

##### Use water-safety measures

[Not implemented](#)

Select appropriate flotation, supervision, and activity limitations while recognizing the limits of swimming ability.

##### Respond to a person in danger in water

[Not implemented](#)

Call for help and use safe reach-or-throw assistance where appropriate without putting an untrained rescuer into the water.

#### Weather and Nature Hazards

Skills: 6

[Not implemented](#)

Outdoor risks vary with temperature, sunlight, storms, terrain, and wildlife; adaptation depends on current conditions and local alerts.

##### Prevent heat-related illness

[Not implemented](#)

Adjust clothing, activity, shade, cooling and fluids to temperature and exertion to reduce overheating; distinguish prevention from first aid after heat illness begins.

##### Protect against harmful UV exposure

[Not implemented](#)

Choose suitable shade, clothing, eye protection and sunscreen practices according to ultraviolet exposure, recognizing that sunburn and eye damage do not depend on high air temperature.

##### Prevent cold exposure injury

[Not implemented](#)

Account for wet clothing, wind, duration, suitable insulation, and warning signs of harmful cooling.

##### Thunderstorm safety

[Not implemented](#)

Choose safer shelter and avoid exposed locations and dangerous structures when lightning threatens.

##### Wildlife and plant safety

[Not implemented](#)

Respect distance and local guidance for bites, stings, poisonous plants, and animal encounters without attempting unnecessary contact.

##### Safe navigation in unfamiliar terrain

[Not implemented](#)

Consider route reliability, weather change, communications, and the limits of personal capability before and during an outing.

### Emergencies and Civil Protection

Groups: 4 · Skills: 16

[Not implemented](#)

Collective hazards can overwhelm normal routines and require verified alerts, protective shelter, evacuation, and coordination with responders. Local regulations and emergency numbers belong to contextual lessons, not permanent country-specific Skill identities.

#### Preparedness and Alerts

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Effective readiness considers local hazards, people requiring assistance, communication failures, and the difference between a warning and an instruction.

##### Understand emergency alerts

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish authoritative warnings and protective instructions from rumours, outdated messages, or unverified reposts.

##### Develop an emergency plan

[Not implemented](#)

Identify meeting points, accessible exits, contacts, responsibilities, and alternatives if communication or transport fails.

##### Prepare essential emergency supplies

[Not implemented](#)

Match water, medicine, documents, lighting, and other essentials to plausible disruption and household needs.

##### Contact emergency services

[Not implemented](#)

Communicate location, type of emergency, persons affected and critical changes clearly, including during medical first aid, while following dispatcher instructions and ensuring the caller's own safety.

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

The correct protective action may be evacuation or remaining under cover, depending on the hazard and official guidance.

##### Choose evacuation or shelter

[Not implemented](#)

Interpret hazard-specific directions and avoid assuming evacuation is always safer than remaining in place.

##### Evacuate safely

[Not implemented](#)

Use an available route, account for smoke or structural danger, and assist others without entering a hazardous area.

##### Shelter during immediate danger

[Not implemented](#)

Recognize suitable protected locations and conditions under which a shelter becomes unsafe.

#### Natural and Technological Disasters

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Floods, earthquakes, chemical releases, and radiation incidents create different exposure pathways and require different protective behaviour.

##### Flood safety

[Not implemented](#)

Avoid floodwater and unstable crossings, recognize sudden changes in water levels, and follow verified evacuation guidance.

##### Respond safely to an earthquake

[Not implemented](#)

Recognize immediate hazards from shaking and falling objects, use appropriate protective actions and consider aftershocks and structural damage before moving or re-entering unsafe spaces.

##### Chemical incident protection

[Not implemented](#)

##### Radiological emergency protection

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish external radiation exposure and contamination, following official instructions on shelter, movement, and decontamination.

#### Conflict-Related Dangers

Skills: 5

[Not implemented](#)

Armed conflict can produce immediate threats and leave dangerous remnants; civil protection centres on avoidance, shelter, and reporting rather than handling weapons.

##### Respond to attack warnings

[Not implemented](#)

Use authoritative warnings and preidentified protective locations, accounting for obstacles and people needing assistance.

##### Respond to an immediate attack

[Not implemented](#)

Recognize the need for immediate protection if an explosion, gunfire or other attack occurs without warning; seek the safest available cover or shelter and follow verified local civil-protection guidance when feasible.

##### Recognize explosive ordnance risk

[Not implemented](#)

##### Respond to a suspected explosive object

[Not implemented](#)

Avoid approaching, touching, or moving it; withdraw safely when possible and report its location through authorized services.
