# Physics — Skills Taxonomy

Date: 2026-10-29

**Approved** subject-specific Physics Skills Taxonomy. Each Skill describes an independent physics competency without imposing limits on the depth of mastery, age, Curriculum level, permissible formulas, teaching method, or assessment approach. Different independent curricula may include the same Skill at different depths and in different sequences; no single curriculum sets the boundaries of the subject catalog.

Physics describes the laws governing natural phenomena and explains principles used in technology. Designing, selecting components for, and optimizing devices belong to the Engineering textbook. General scientific methodology belongs to Human and Thinking. Cross-subject references to these topics do not establish shared global Skill IDs.

**Approved subject boundaries:** Mechanics covers the physical principles of simple machines, forces, equilibrium, and structural stability, while the design and optimization of machines and structures belong to Engineering. Waves covers general wave phenomena; Optics covers light propagation and image formation; Radiation covers electromagnetic-spectrum phenomena and interactions with matter; Thermodynamics covers radiative heat transfer alongside conduction and convection. Conservation of linear momentum, angular momentum, and electric charge are placed in their respective subject groups, not in the Energy Domain.

References: [OpenStax University Physics](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/preface), [OpenStax: Wave Interaction](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/13-3-wave-interaction-superposition-and-interference), [Studio Subject Proposition](../../../../studio/Subject%20Proposition.md). These materials were used to check subject coverage, not as a ready-made curriculum or a literal structure for Svitlo.

## DOMAINS

### Measurement

Groups: 2 · Skills: 7

[Not implemented](#)

Physical measurement connects an observable property to an agreed method of comparison through the choice of quantity, unit, and instrument. Its purpose is to make descriptions of phenomena reproducible without tying them to formulas or mathematics lessons.

#### Physical Quantities and Units

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

A single object can be described using different quantities; comparisons of the same quantity require compatible units and observation conditions.

##### Distinguish a physical quantity from its unit of measurement

[Not implemented](#)

A physical quantity describes a property of a body or phenomenon, whereas a unit establishes an agreed scale for expressing it numerically; length and centimeter serve different roles.

##### Select a unit for a familiar physical quantity

[Not implemented](#)

Choose appropriate units of time, length, and mass in everyday examples without confusing a physical quantity with an object.

##### Compare measurements of the same quantity

[Not implemented](#)

Given consistent units and clearly stated conditions, determine which measured value is larger or smaller; this is physical interpretation, not arithmetic practice.

##### Explain the physical meaning of a body's mass

[Not implemented](#)

Mass characterizes a body's inertia: under the same applied influence, a more massive body is generally harder to change in motion. Mass must not be equated with gravitational force, which can depend on location.

#### Instruments and Measurement Methods

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

An instrument's reading depends on its scale, units, and correct use; the method of obtaining data matters as much as the number itself.

##### Read temperature from a thermometer

[Not implemented](#)

Relate the position of a pointer or liquid column to the temperature scale and units, accounting for the scale origin and measurement limits.

##### Read a simple measuring instrument

[Not implemented](#)

Correctly relate the measured quantity to the position of a pointer on a scale, its units, and the zero reference; a reading has physical meaning only under the relevant measurement conditions.

##### Explain why repeated measurements may differ

[Not implemented](#)

Repeated measurements can differ because of instrument precision limits, reading methods, or changing conditions; a difference in values does not always mean that the measured phenomenon has changed.

### Mechanics

Groups: 6 · Skills: 21

[Not implemented](#)

Mechanics describes changes in bodies' positions over time, the inertial properties of motion, and the transmission of mechanical action through simple systems. Basic explanations of types of forces and equilibrium are assigned to the neighboring Domain.

#### Describing Motion

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Position, direction, speed, and repetition are needed for a qualitative description of a trajectory; motion is always considered relative to a chosen reference.

##### Identify motion relative to a reference point

[Not implemented](#)

Motion is a change in a body's position relative to a reference frame over time; an object can move relative to one reference and remain stationary relative to another.

##### Describe the direction of motion

[Not implemented](#)

Determine the direction of a body's velocity or displacement relative to a selected reference frame, distinguishing direction from the total distance traveled.

##### Compare speeds of motion

[Not implemented](#)

Speed relates displacement or distance to time; comparisons account for whether the same time intervals or different periods of motion are involved.

##### Recognize periodic motion

[Not implemented](#)

Identify recurring physical states and distinguish regular cycles of motion from random or one-time changes in position.

#### Inertia and Changes in Motion

Skills: 2

[Not implemented](#)

A body may continue moving after an applied action ceases, while changes in motion are associated with interaction; accessible models do not require equations of motion.

##### Recognize manifestations of inertia in motion

[Not implemented](#)

Explain why an object or passenger tends to maintain its previous state of motion during an abrupt change in speed.

##### Distinguish uniform from nonuniform motion

[Not implemented](#)

Compare successive positions of an object to determine whether it travels equal distances in equal time intervals.

#### Simple Machines

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Levers, pulleys, wheels, and gears demonstrate the transmission of force and rotation. The focus here is on physical principles, not selecting a design or engineering a machine.

##### Explain how a lever works

[Not implemented](#)

A lever's mechanical advantage depends on distances between the fulcrum and the points where forces are applied; gaining force involves a corresponding change in displacement.

##### Explain how a wheel and axle work

[Not implemented](#)

The shared rotation of a wheel and axle transmits motion and changes the relationship between applied forces and displacements; a wheel can also change the nature of friction.

##### Explain how a pulley works

[Not implemented](#)

A fixed pulley changes the direction of the applied force, whereas a movable pulley can reduce the force needed by distributing the load across rope segments.

##### Predict the rotation of meshed gears

[Not implemented](#)

A directly meshed pair of gears rotates in opposite directions, and the ratio of their angular velocities depends on the number of teeth.

#### Newton's Laws

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

Newton's laws establish the relationships among forces, inertia, and changes in bodies' motion in the appropriate reference frames.

##### Explain Newton's first law

[Not implemented](#)

When the net force is zero, a body's velocity in an inertial reference frame remains constant; rest is a special case.

##### Explain Newton's second law

[Not implemented](#)

A body's acceleration is determined by the net force and its mass; under the same force, bodies of different masses change velocity differently.

##### Explain Newton's third law

[Not implemented](#)

The interaction forces between two bodies are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction but act on different bodies, so they do not cancel on a single body.

#### Momentum and Collisions

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Momentum characterizes a body's motion, while collisions demonstrate momentum transfer and change. In the absence of external impulse, the total momentum of a system is conserved regardless of the type of collision.

##### Explain linear momentum

[Not implemented](#)

Momentum depends on a body's mass and velocity and has a direction; equal velocities of bodies with different masses do not imply equal momentum.

##### Explain impulse

[Not implemented](#)

A change in a body's momentum is related to the magnitude and duration of an applied force, which explains physical differences among braking methods.

##### Distinguish elastic from inelastic collisions

[Not implemented](#)

In an elastic collision, the system's kinetic energy is conserved; in an inelastic collision, some becomes internal energy and deformation. Momentum remains conserved in an isolated system.

##### Explain conservation of linear momentum

[Not implemented](#)

In the absence of a net external impulse, the total momentum of a system remains constant even though individual bodies change their momenta.

#### Rotational Motion

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Rotation involves torque, mass distribution, and inertial properties. Conservation of angular momentum describes a system's motion when there is no net external torque.

##### Explain torque

[Not implemented](#)

The turning effect of a force depends on its direction and lever arm relative to the axis, not merely on the force's magnitude.

##### Explain rotational inertia

[Not implemented](#)

The distribution of mass relative to an axis affects how rotation changes, even when the total mass is the same.

##### Distinguish rotation about an axis from motion along a circle

[Not implemented](#)

A body's rotation about its own axis differs from the motion of its center along a circular path, during which the direction of velocity changes.

##### Explain conservation of angular momentum

[Not implemented](#)

In the absence of net external torque, a system's total angular momentum is conserved.

### Forces and Interactions

Groups: 3 · Skills: 14

[Not implemented](#)

When bodies touch or affect one another at a distance, their motion, shape, or state of equilibrium may change. This Domain covers general concepts of force, contact, gravity, and stability; specifically magnetic and electric phenomena belong to Electromagnetism.

#### Contact Forces and Friction

Skills: 5

[Not implemented](#)

Contact can produce pushing, pulling, and friction; the direction of interaction and surface properties affect motion.

##### Recognize pushing forces

[Not implemented](#)

A contact push transfers force from the acting body in the direction of pressure on another body; not every change in motion is caused by pushing.

##### Recognize pulling forces

[Not implemented](#)

Pulling is a contact interaction through a connection or grip, with force directed toward its source; it differs from pressure and pushing.

##### Predict the effect of force direction

[Not implemented](#)

The direction of the net force relative to velocity determines whether motion speeds up, slows down, or changes direction.

##### Compare motion on different surfaces

[Not implemented](#)

Interactions between contacting surfaces affect friction and resistance to sliding or rolling, changing mechanical motion under otherwise similar conditions.

##### Explain the role of friction in stopping

[Not implemented](#)

Friction during sliding or rolling can reduce kinetic energy of motion, converting it into the internal energy of the bodies and surroundings.

#### Equilibrium and Stability

Skills: 6

[Not implemented](#)

Balanced forces allow a body to remain at rest, while the arrangement of supports and loads affects a model's stability and deformation.

##### Recognize balanced forces

[Not implemented](#)

When the net force is zero, there is no acceleration; a body may be at rest or continue moving uniformly in a straight line.

##### Identify structural support elements

[Not implemented](#)

Identify the elements supporting a model bridge or building.

##### Predict structural stability

[Not implemented](#)

Compare the stability of two models using basic support-related characteristics.

##### Explain load transfer

[Not implemented](#)

Identify how forces from a load pass through structural elements to supports, and distinguish the force-transmission path from the shape of the structure.

##### Compare the strength of structural shapes

[Not implemented](#)

Under identical testing conditions, determine which shape better withstands a load.

##### Predict the effect of center-of-mass position on stability

[Not implemented](#)

The position of the center of mass relative to the support area affects whether a body can overturn under gravity.

#### Gravity

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

Bodies with mass interact gravitationally even without touching. Earth's gravity governs falling near its surface, and other celestial bodies also exert gravitational influence.

##### Predict the direction of falling

[Not implemented](#)

Near Earth's surface, gravity acts approximately toward Earth's center, so a body in free fall accelerates downward relative to the local vertical.

##### Distinguish a body's mass from Earth's gravitational force

[Not implemented](#)

Compare a body's unchanged mass with gravitational force, which depends on the gravitational field, using models. Do not equate these quantities or confuse gravitational force with the body's weight as the force it exerts on a support.

##### Explain Earth's gravitational force on a body

[Not implemented](#)

Earth exerts a gravitational force on a body depending on the body's mass and the gravitational field; distinguish that force from mass and from weight as the body's action on a support.

### Fluid Mechanics

Groups: 4 · Skills: 15

[Not implemented](#)

Water and air transmit pressure, flow, resist bodies' motion, and can provide buoyancy. Familiar containers, floating objects, and airflow make these phenomena accessible.

#### Pressure in Liquids and Gases

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Pressure is transmitted through a medium and changes with depth in still water; container examples help distinguish pressure from the mere amount of water.

##### Explain why water pressure increases with depth

[Not implemented](#)

In a stationary liquid of constant density, pressure increases with depth because of the weight of the layers above; the container's shape alone does not determine it.

##### Recognize liquid pressure acting in different directions

[Not implemented](#)

Use a model of holes in a container to explain why water exerts pressure in directions other than downward.

##### Identify gas pressure in examples

[Not implemented](#)

A gas acts on surfaces through collisions of its particles; pressure depends on the gas's physical state and the conditions of its confinement.

##### Explain pressure transmission in an enclosed liquid

[Not implemented](#)

A pressure change applied to an enclosed liquid at rest is transmitted in every direction, unlike the variation of hydrostatic pressure with depth.

#### Floating and Buoyancy

Skills: 5

[Not implemented](#)

A body's behavior in water is determined by gravity together with forces exerted by the liquid; material and shape may affect the outcome through average density.

##### Distinguish rising, floating, and sinking

[Not implemented](#)

Identify three possible behaviors of safe objects in water through observation or simulation.

##### Predict how a load affects floating

[Not implemented](#)

Increasing the mass of a load changes the buoyant force and displaced volume needed for equilibrium, so a body may become more deeply submerged.

##### Explain density

[Not implemented](#)

Density is the mass per unit volume of a substance and helps determine the relationship between a body's properties and those of the surrounding medium.

##### Explain buoyant force

[Not implemented](#)

The pressure difference exerted by a liquid or gas on a submerged body produces a net upward force.

##### Explain Archimedes' principle

[Not implemented](#)

Under the relevant conditions, the buoyant force equals the weight of the volume of liquid or gas displaced by the body.

#### Aerodynamics

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

Air exerts drag on moving bodies; shape and relative speed affect the interaction. Building aircraft or designing hulls are Engineering applications.

##### Predict the effect of shape on motion through air

[Not implemented](#)

A body's shape and orientation affect airflow around it, drag, and, under certain conditions, lift.

##### Explain the origin of air resistance during motion

[Not implemented](#)

Using a paper sheet or ball, connect drag to the body's interaction with air; do not claim that drag arises only from an unsuitable shape.

##### Explain aerodynamic lift

[Not implemented](#)

Airflow interacting with a body can produce a force perpendicular to the flow; it should not be explained solely by differences in air-path length.

#### Hydrodynamics

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

Water and other liquids flow under certain conditions and resist bodies moving through the stream; comparison with air explains differences between the media.

##### Compare resistance to motion in water and air

[Not implemented](#)

Use qualitative examples to recognize why moving the same object through water and through air feels different.

##### Explain water flow between containers at different levels

[Not implemented](#)

A hydrostatic pressure difference between connected vessels causes liquid to flow until pressures balance or other forces intervene.

##### Explain continuity of incompressible-fluid flow

[Not implemented](#)

For steady flow without leakage, a change in a channel's cross-sectional area is associated with a change in fluid speed.

### Energy

Groups: 4 · Skills: 12

[Not implemented](#)

Energy is a quantity that helps describe physical changes and connect phenomena involving motion, heating, light, and electricity. The emphasis is on energy transfer, transformation, and conservation rather than arithmetic formulas.

#### Forms of Energy

Skills: 5

[Not implemented](#)

Motion, position, deformation, and internal state can be associated with different forms of energy; descriptions should refer to a specific physical system.

##### Classify forms of energy

[Not implemented](#)

Distinguish energy of motion, energy related to bodies' positions, elastic deformation energy, and internal energy based on the physical properties of a system.

##### Distinguish energy from its source

[Not implemented](#)

Energy characterizes the state and changes of a system, whereas a source is a body or process from which energy is transferred to another system; the source does not create it from nothing.

##### Explain kinetic energy as energy of motion

[Not implemented](#)

Kinetic energy is associated with a body's motion relative to a reference frame and depends on mass and speed, but it is not the same as momentum.

##### Explain potential energy

[Not implemented](#)

Potential energy is related to a physical system's configuration and interactions among its components, including bodies' positions in a gravitational field or elastic deformation. Its value is specified relative to a chosen zero level; this competency addresses physical meaning rather than calculation.

##### Calculate gravitational potential energy

[Not implemented](#)

Determine the change in a body's gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface from its mass, gravitational acceleration, and change in height, correctly accounting for the chosen reference level and units. This calculation assumes an approximately uniform gravitational field and differs from calculating elastic potential energy.

#### Energy Transfer and Transformation

Skills: 2

[Not implemented](#)

Energy can pass between objects and change form; the sequence of events matters more than the name of a particular device.

##### Identify energy transfer between bodies

[Not implemented](#)

Using colliding balls or water heating, show which body transfers energy to which other body.

##### Explain energy transformation in a physical process

[Not implemented](#)

Energy changes form during physical processes, for example when energy shifts between motion and position; the total energy of an isolated system is conserved.

#### Conservation of Energy

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

The total energy of an isolated system is conserved even as its form or distribution among bodies changes. A decrease in energy available for useful work is not its disappearance; for an open system, exchange with the surroundings must be considered.

##### Explain the law of conservation of energy

[Not implemented](#)

The total energy of an isolated system remains unchanged; for an open system, energy exchange with the surroundings must be taken into account.

##### Explain the loss of useful energy

[Not implemented](#)

Dissipation into forms of energy less suitable for a particular task reduces a process's usefulness but does not violate conservation of total energy.

##### Refute the idea that energy disappears

[Not implemented](#)

Identify unaccounted energy transfers and transformations instead of attributing a system's change of state to energy disappearing.

#### Work and Power

Skills: 2

[Not implemented](#)

Work describes energy transfer by a force during displacement, while power describes the rate of energy transfer.

##### Explain mechanical work

[Not implemented](#)

Mechanical work involves the component of force along displacement; exerting force without displacement does not perform mechanical work.

##### Explain the physical meaning of power

[Not implemented](#)

Power describes the rate of doing work or transferring energy and is not a separate form of energy.

### Waves

Groups: 4 · Skills: 23

[Not implemented](#)

Oscillations produce recurring disturbances that can propagate through water, air, and other media. Sound is one manifestation, while wave patterns are visible on the surface of water.

#### Oscillations

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Repetitive motion around a given state allows vibration, periodicity, and sound sources to be described.

##### Recognize oscillatory motion

[Not implemented](#)

Oscillation is a change in a physical state around equilibrium that may recur; not every motion involving reversals is harmonic oscillation.

##### Relate sound to vibration

[Not implemented](#)

Sound arises when an oscillating source disturbs an elastic medium; oscillation without a medium does not produce a mechanical sound wave.

##### Detect vibrations of a sound source

[Not implemented](#)

Identify the oscillating part of a system that generates a sound disturbance, as distinct from components that merely transmit vibration onward.

##### Recognize the periodicity of oscillations

[Not implemented](#)

A periodic oscillation repeats a physical state at equal time intervals; not every motion that changes direction is periodic.

#### Wave Properties

Skills: 6

[Not implemented](#)

Waves have a direction of propagation, a characteristic rhythm, and a disturbance magnitude; observing water waves distinguishes wave propagation from transport of all the water.

##### Distinguish source oscillation from wave propagation

[Not implemented](#)

An oscillating source creates a disturbance that propagates through a medium, while the medium's particles do not necessarily move along with the wavefront.

##### Compare wave frequencies by observable rhythm

[Not implemented](#)

In two animations, identify which produces more recurring crests in the same amount of time.

##### Explain wave amplitude

[Not implemented](#)

Amplitude is the greatest deviation of a physical quantity from its equilibrium value, not the wave's frequency.

##### Explain wavelength

[Not implemented](#)

Wavelength is the distance between adjacent points at the same phase of the disturbance, such as two successive crests.

##### Explain wave speed

[Not implemented](#)

Wave speed describes the propagation of a disturbance and differs from the motion of particles within the medium.

##### Distinguish wave energy transfer from matter transport

[Not implemented](#)

A wave carries energy without necessarily transporting the entire medium over the corresponding distance.

#### Acoustics

Skills: 8

[Not implemented](#)

Loudness, pitch, and the propagation of sound through a medium are related to oscillation properties; auditory experiences can be explored with safe simulations.

##### Compare the loudness of sounds

[Not implemented](#)

Loudness is the perception of sound intensity and also depends on frequency and hearing sensitivity; compare it independently of pitch.

##### Distinguish high-pitched from low-pitched sounds

[Not implemented](#)

Pitch is primarily related to the frequency of sound oscillations, not amplitude, which affects intensity.

##### Recognize sound transmission through a medium

[Not implemented](#)

Sound requires an elastic material medium, in which pressure or deformation oscillations pass between adjacent parts of matter; mechanical sound does not propagate in a vacuum.

##### Explain sound attenuation with distance

[Not implemented](#)

As a wave spreads from a localized source, its energy is generally distributed over an increasing area, reducing intensity; absorption also affects sound levels.

##### Explain an echo as sound reflection

[Not implemented](#)

Using an echo in an empty room, recognize a returning sound wave after it interacts with a surface.

##### Explain the change in perceived pitch during relative motion of source and listener

[Not implemented](#)

For an unchanged sound emitted by a source, explain the Doppler effect: the frequency heard by the observer is higher as the source approaches and lower as it moves away. Do not confuse this frequency change with loudness or engine revolutions.

##### Explain how sound pitch depends on oscillation frequency

[Not implemented](#)

All else being equal, a higher sound frequency corresponds to a higher pitch; it is not the same as increased loudness.

##### Explain how sound loudness depends on oscillation amplitude

[Not implemented](#)

All else being equal, a greater sound-pressure amplitude means greater intensity, although perceived loudness also depends on frequency.

#### Wave Phenomena

Skills: 5

[Not implemented](#)

Reflection, refraction, interference, diffraction, and resonance can occur in different kinds of physical waves.

##### Explain wave reflection

[Not implemented](#)

Part of a disturbance may return from a boundary between media, changing its direction of propagation.

##### Explain wave refraction

[Not implemented](#)

A change in propagation speed at a boundary between media can change a wave's direction while the source frequency stays the same.

##### Explain wave interference

[Not implemented](#)

Superposed waves may reinforce or weaken the resulting disturbance depending on their phases.

##### Explain wave diffraction

[Not implemented](#)

Waves may bend around obstacles or spread after passing through openings depending on relative dimensions and wavelength.

##### Explain resonance

[Not implemented](#)

A periodic driving force near a system's natural frequency can substantially increase the amplitude of its oscillations.

### Thermodynamics

Groups: 4 · Skills: 15

[Not implemented](#)

Thermal processes describe changes in temperature, energy transfer, and transitions between states of matter. Macroscopic observations are distinguished from particle-level explanations in the neighboring Domain.

#### Temperature and Heating

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Temperature characterizes thermal state but is not the same as a subjective feeling of warmth or cold; heating and cooling are changes of state.

##### Compare objects' temperatures

[Not implemented](#)

Temperature characterizes a body's thermal state; valid comparisons rely on measurements, not the intensity of subjective warmth.

##### Distinguish temperature from the sensation of warmth

[Not implemented](#)

Explain why the sensation of touch is not always an accurate measure of temperature.

##### Recognize heating

[Not implemented](#)

An increase in temperature reflects a change in thermal state, but energy input does not always increase temperature during a phase transition.

##### Recognize cooling

[Not implemented](#)

A decrease in temperature can accompany energy transfer to the surroundings or changes in a system's physical state.

#### Heat-Transfer Mechanisms

Skills: 5

[Not implemented](#)

Thermal energy is transferred through particle interactions, liquid or gas motion, or electromagnetic radiation. Several mechanisms may act simultaneously in a single process.

##### Predict the direction of heat transfer

[Not implemented](#)

In the absence of external work, heat spontaneously flows from a warmer body to a colder one until thermal equilibrium is reached.

##### Explain conduction as energy transfer through matter

[Not implemented](#)

Using a metal spoon heated in hot water, explain energy transfer without bulk movement of the entire substance.

##### Explain convection as energy transfer by fluid flow

[Not implemented](#)

Convection carries thermal energy with macroscopic motion of a liquid or gas; distinguish it from conduction without bulk material flow.

##### Explain radiative heat transfer

[Not implemented](#)

Using heat from the Sun or fire, demonstrate energy transfer by electromagnetic waves without contact or moving air.

##### Identify the heat-transfer mechanism from a physical situation

[Not implemented](#)

For a hot spoon, heated water, or sunlight, select and justify conduction, convection, radiation, or a combination of them. This is an integrative competency, not three separate tests combined in one task.

#### Thermal Insulation

Skills: 2

[Not implemented](#)

Insulating materials slow down energy transfer but do not create energy themselves; this differs from describing the three mechanisms of heat exchange.

##### Compare materials' thermal-insulation properties

[Not implemented](#)

Determine from experimental results which material slows heat transfer more effectively.

##### Select a material for maintaining temperature

[Not implemented](#)

Choose an insulator for a simple everyday task and justify the choice using the material's properties.

#### Phase Transitions

Skills: 4

[Not implemented](#)

Melting, freezing, evaporation, and condensation link thermal conditions to changes of state of the same substance.

##### Distinguish melting from freezing of water

[Not implemented](#)

Melting changes a substance from solid to liquid, while freezing is the reverse; both are phase transitions of the same substance.

##### Explain evaporation

[Not implemented](#)

Evaporation is the transition of particles from the surface of a liquid into the gas phase and can occur without boiling.

##### Explain condensation

[Not implemented](#)

Condensation is the transition of matter from gas to liquid, including the formation of droplets from water vapor.

##### Distinguish evaporation from boiling

[Not implemented](#)

Boiling produces vapor bubbles throughout the volume of a liquid, whereas evaporation occurs at its surface.

### Molecular Physics

Groups: 3 · Skills: 10

[Not implemented](#)

Particle models help explain physical properties and changes in states of matter. Unlike chemistry, this Domain focuses on particle movement, interactions, and arrangement rather than chemical reactions.

#### Particle Model of Matter

Skills: 7

[Not implemented](#)

Substances consist of particles with spaces and interactions between them; the model explains properties without requiring complex formulas.

##### Explain the particulate structure of matter

[Not implemented](#)

Demonstrate that even water in a transparent glass consists of particles invisible to the naked eye.

##### Compare particle arrangements in solids, liquids, and gases

[Not implemented](#)

Particles in solids mostly oscillate around fixed positions; in liquids, particles can move relative to one another; and in gases they have much greater freedom of movement and characteristic interparticle distances.

##### Relate particle motion to temperature changes

[Not implemented](#)

Using an animation, explain why average particle motion generally becomes more vigorous when heated.

##### Explain random thermal motion of particles

[Not implemented](#)

Particles of matter are constantly moving, but not all at the same speed; the type of motion depends on the physical state.

##### Explain gas pressure through particle motion

[Not implemented](#)

The combined collisions of particles with the vessel walls produce force per unit area, that is, pressure.

##### Explain thermal expansion through particle motion and interactions

[Not implemented](#)

Heating often changes the average distances between particles, not the sizes of the molecules themselves.

##### Explain the properties of states of matter through particle motion

[Not implemented](#)

Differences in the arrangement, motion, and interactions of particles explain the properties of solids, liquids, and gases.

#### Diffusion and Mixing

Skills: 1

[Not implemented](#)

Random thermal motion can cause particles to spread gradually without deliberate stirring.

##### Explain diffusion

[Not implemented](#)

Diffusion results from particles' thermal motion and the gradual equalization of their concentrations without the need for macroscopic mixing.

#### Gas Properties

Skills: 2

[Not implemented](#)

The compressibility of gases and the dependence of their pressure on conditions follow from particle motion and the spaces between particles.

##### Explain gas compressibility

[Not implemented](#)

Compressing a gas reduces the average distances between particles while the sizes of the molecules remain virtually unchanged.

##### Explain how gas pressure depends on temperature at constant volume

[Not implemented](#)

At constant volume, increasing a gas's temperature generally increases its pressure because the particles move more vigorously.

### Electromagnetism

Groups: 8 · Skills: 31

[Not implemented](#)

Electric charges, currents, and magnetic fields describe related interactions; electricity and magnetism share underlying principles but begin with different observations.

#### Electric Charges and Interactions

Skills: 3

[Not implemented](#)

Electrification, interactions between charged bodies, and charge transfer obey conservation of electric charge. Electric interaction can occur without direct contact.

##### Recognize the charging of objects by friction

[Not implemented](#)

Using a safe balloon or comb model, connect friction with electrostatic effects rather than attributing them to magnetic attraction.

##### Distinguish attraction from repulsion between electric charges

[Not implemented](#)

Like charges repel and unlike charges attract; electric interaction is distinct from magnetic interaction.

##### Explain conservation of electric charge

[Not implemented](#)

The total charge of an isolated system is conserved during charge transfer and physical interactions.

#### Electric Circuits

Skills: 9

[Not implemented](#)

An electric circuit needs a conducting path, a source, and components whose interactions determine the operation of a safe model.

##### Identify an electrical power source

[Not implemented](#)

A source maintains a potential difference between its terminals and converts other forms of energy into electrical energy, unlike a passive conductor.

##### Identify a consumer of electrical energy

[Not implemented](#)

An electrical load converts received electrical energy into thermal, mechanical, light, or other forms, rather than merely "using current."

##### Identify the function of a switch

[Not implemented](#)

A switch changes the state of a conducting path and can interrupt current in a particular branch, but it is not itself an energy source.

##### Recognize a closed electric circuit

[Not implemented](#)

A closed circuit has a continuous conducting path; sustained current also requires a source of electromotive force.

##### Explain an open circuit

[Not implemented](#)

Opening a branch interrupts its conducting path; in a circuit with parallel branches, other parts may continue to operate.

##### Predict the effect of a switch in a circuit

[Not implemented](#)

The consequences of switching depend on where the switch is placed relative to the source, loads, and parallel paths.

##### Explain series connections of electric circuit components

[Not implemented](#)

Components connected in series share a common current path: the same current passes through each, and the total voltage across the combination is the sum of the voltages across the individual components. Breaking the single path stops current through all those components.

##### Explain parallel connections of electric circuit components

[Not implemented](#)

Parallel branches connect to the same two nodes, so each has the same voltage and the total current is divided among the branches. Opening one branch does not necessarily stop current in the others.

##### Explain current balance at a circuit node

[Not implemented](#)

Under steady current, charge does not accumulate at a node; the total current entering equals the total current leaving.

#### Conductors and Insulators
