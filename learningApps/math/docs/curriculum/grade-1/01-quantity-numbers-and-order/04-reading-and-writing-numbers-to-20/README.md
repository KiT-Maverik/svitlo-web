# Reading and Writing Numbers to 20

> **Curriculum: Mathematics — Grade 1 · Quantity, Numbers, and Order · Lesson 04**
> **Status:** Methodological Learning Brief awaiting editorial review; not an implemented lesson, screenplay, or Assessment Specification.

**Learning objective:** Read, write, and identify numbers from 0 to 20.

**Target audience:** Beginning mathematics learners, approximately ages 6–7. This age estimate is not an access restriction.

## Target Skills

- [Reading, writing and matching numbers 0–20](../../../../../src/data/skillsTaxonomy/numeration/notation/skills/readWrite20.data.ts)

## Prerequisites

Recognizing digits 0–9 and counting fluently forward through twenty.

## Expected learning outcomes

After learning the material, the learner:

- Read written numerals from 0 to 20.
- Write dictated values as numerals without reversing the digits.
- Match a numeral to a collection of the corresponding size.
- Distinguish a two-digit numeral from a single digit symbol.

## Mathematical content

- Each value from zero through twenty has a conventional written numeral.
- Numbers 10–20 use two digits; a lone digit 1 is not the same numeral as 11.
- Spoken name, written numeral, and concrete quantity denote the same mathematical value.

## Scope and boundaries

Keep the range at 0–20. Formal explanation of decimal place values belongs to another competency.

## Typical misconceptions and risks

- Reversing digits in two-digit numerals.
- Confusing ten, eleven, and twelve when matching spoken forms to written notation.
- Reading each digit separately instead of interpreting the whole numeral.

## Indicators of learning objective attainment

These are **pedagogical guidelines for future assessment design**, not finalized questions, scoring thresholds, or mastery judgments.

- Reads and writes arbitrary numbers within the range, not just ordered samples.
- Matches written values to collections correctly.
- Recognizes an incorrectly written or read number.

## Connection to learning sequence

Written numerals support distinctions between cardinality, ordinal position, and numerical neighbors.
